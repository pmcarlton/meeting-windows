# Geographic data

`dist/map-data.js` contains timezone boundaries derived from [Timezone Boundary Builder, release 2026e](https://github.com/evansiroky/timezone-boundary-builder/releases/tag/2026e), based on © OpenStreetMap contributors. This derived boundary database is available under the [Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). Original download: `timezones-with-oceans.geojson.zip` in that release. Boundary rings were simplified with a radial prepass and Douglas–Peucker tolerance of 0.13 degrees, rounded after projection, and converted to SVG paths. Map coordinates use x = (longitude + 180) × 4 and y = (sin(84°) − sin(latitude)) × 108 + 22. The app can interpolate these paths toward evenly spaced latitudes during zoom. The inverse projection uses the bundled rounded coordinates; this changes spacing but does not restore geographic detail lost during simplification. Political boundaries and small islands are approximate at this scale; conversion uses the selected IANA timezone's browser rules.

The coastline in that file comes from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/), public domain, via `ne_110m_land.geojson` in [natural-earth-vector](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector).

`dist/cities.js` contains 34,173 populated places from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/), downloaded from [cities15000.zip](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/cities15000.zip), under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Fields retained: display name, ASCII name when different, country code, first-level administrative code, IANA timezone, latitude, longitude, population. Coordinates are rounded to three decimals; records are sorted by descending population. Retrieved October 19, 2026.

The map and search datasets are bundled locally; the app makes no runtime geocoding or map requests. Current UTC offsets and daylight-saving conversions come from the browser's timezone database, rather than the map colors or city data.

Viewport focus decodes the bundled projected rings back to approximate longitude/latitude and rotates their spherical geometry. Degenerate rings are discarded; outer rings and holes are reoriented for D3's spherical convention. It uses a cylindrical `y = tanh(2.12 × latitude) / 2.12` mapping (radians), with matching horizontal and vertical scale at its central great circle. Geometry is clipped at the rotated seam and the viewport. This lens changes area away from the focus. It cannot restore detail or latitude precision removed from the original map data.

Bundled libraries: d3-array 3.2.4 and d3-geo 3.1.1, by Mike Bostock and contributors, under ISC licenses. Their full notices are included in `dist/vendor/`. The app loads the bundled copies locally; it does not need a CDN or a map service at runtime.
