# Geographic data

`map-data.js` contains timezone boundaries derived from [Timezone Boundary Builder, release 2026e](https://github.com/evansiroky/timezone-boundary-builder/releases/tag/2026e), based on © OpenStreetMap contributors. This derived boundary database is available under the [Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). Original download: `timezones-with-oceans.geojson.zip` in that release. Boundary rings were simplified with a radial prepass and Douglas–Peucker tolerance of 0.13 degrees, rounded after projection, and converted to SVG paths. Map coordinates use x = (longitude + 180) × 4 and y = (sin(84°) − sin(latitude)) × 108 + 22. Political boundaries and small islands are approximate at this scale; conversion uses the selected IANA timezone's browser rules.

The coastline in that file comes from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/), public domain, via `ne_110m_land.geojson` in [natural-earth-vector](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector).

`cities.js` contains 34,173 populated places from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/), downloaded from [cities15000.zip](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/cities15000.zip), under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Fields retained: display name, ASCII name when different, country code, first-level administrative code, IANA timezone, latitude, longitude, population. Coordinates are rounded to three decimals; records are sorted by descending population. Retrieved October 19, 2026.

The map and search datasets are bundled locally; the app makes no runtime geocoding or map requests. Current UTC offsets and daylight-saving conversions come from the browser's timezone database, rather than the map colors or city data.
